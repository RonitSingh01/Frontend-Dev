Introduction to Font Families and Weight

This introductory paragraph uses a standard serif font family (like 'Georgia') to convey a classic, academic feel. We are demonstrating different font weights here. The word 'serif' is bolded using a high font-weight, making it stand out from the surrounding text. Typography is about more than just choosing a font; it involves setting the right visual rhythm.

In contrast, this paragraph switches to a sans-serif font (like 'Arial'). Sans-serif fonts are often preferred for screen reading due to their clean lines and modern appearance. Notice how the shape of the letters changes. We can further adjust the text's spacing for improved clarity.

Spacing and Balance: Height and Kerning

Effective text styling relies on balance. The line-height property controls the vertical space between lines of text, which is crucial for long-form reading. A generous line-height prevents text from feeling dense and overwhelming.

Next, consider **letter-spacing** (also known as kerning or tracking in professional tools). By adding or subtracting space between characters, we can alter the visual density. Too much spacing can break word recognition, but a slight positive adjustment can be useful for all-caps titles or specific design elements.

Finally, we look at alignment. The majority of text should be left-aligned for ease of reading, but center or right alignment can be used for short pull quotes or specific design blocks.
